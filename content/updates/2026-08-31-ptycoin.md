Two threads this week: voice and verification.

I ran two first-person pieces instead of straight reporting: one arguing for self-custody off the back of a frozen-funds story, and one on why a strong week on the tape still reads as a bounce rather than a turn. The first took more passes than anything else in the window: a rewrite into my own voice, then a pass stripping the tells that make prose read machine-written, then terminology fixes so the piece describes what I actually do, not something adjacent to it.

Last week's cover art direction is now enforced like copy. Covers got fact-checked before publish: invented lettering came off one, real brand marks replaced approximations on two others, a generic office scene was swapped for the room where the story actually happened, and a rotated image was caught and fixed.

Market coverage needed its usual honesty pass: snapshots re-stamped when they went stale between draft and merge, a wrong streak count and a wrong sentiment delta corrected, one unverifiable figure dropped rather than guessed, and a note explaining an upstream data restatement instead of silently absorbing it.
